Wind energy · 2025

Can wings behind a rotor speed up wake recovery?

  • Wind energy
  • Aerodynamics
  • Flow control

Wings mounted behind a multirotor shed tip vortices that lift slow wake air and bring faster air down from above. Steady CFD, compared with wind-tunnel measurements, tests one-, two- and four-wing layouts; in the modelled cases the wake recovers faster with the wings. The OpenFOAM case represents rotor thrust and wing lift as momentum sources on selected cells.

Flow diagnostics · 2021

How sparse can particle tracks be before vortices are lost?

  • Fluid mechanics
  • Flow diagnostics

Coherent-structure colouring (CSC) groups particle tracks by how their mutual distances change in time. Applied to measured tracks in an Ahmed-body wake and a swirling jet, detection depended mainly on seeding density: a mean particle spacing below about 15 % of the flow’s characteristic length mattered more than track length. The Python code includes the Ahmed-body tracks.

Flow diagnostics · 2021

Do tracers that stay neighbours belong to one structure?

  • Fluid mechanics
  • Dynamical systems
  • Flow diagnostics

Tracers that remain Voronoi neighbours over time tend to move together. The method counts the edges each pair of tracers shares along their tracks and turns this into one spectral colour per tracer, without fixing a number of clusters. It was tested on the double gyre and on sparse three-dimensional measurements behind a bluff body.

Fluid–structure interaction · 2019

How do Reynolds number and damping shape vortex-induced vibration?

  • Fluid mechanics
  • Fluid–structure interaction

A cylinder on springs, free to move across the flow, locks onto its own vortex shedding over a range of flow speeds. Two- and three-dimensional CFD maps how Reynolds number and structural damping change the amplitude branches and the wake modes. The repository writes an equivalent OpenFOAM case from one script.

Structural optimisation · 2019

Can truss sizing with self-weight be solved as a linear problem?

  • Structural mechanics
  • Optimisation

In a statically determinate truss, member forces stay linear in the member areas even with self-weight, so minimum-mass sizing under stress limits becomes a linear problem. The paper tests the method on benchmarks from 10 to 1,240 members. The Python re-implementation sizes the 37-member benchmark to 13.15 kg (paper: 13.23–13.35 kg).

  • Dynamical systems
  • Celestial mechanics

Lid-driven cavity flow

  • Fluid mechanics
  • Numerical methods

A compact incompressible Navier–Stokes solver for the two-dimensional lid-driven cavity: staggered grid, explicit time stepping and a pressure projection solved with a discrete cosine transform. The Re = 1000 solution is checked against the Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982) benchmark.

Scientific software · Rotor aerodynamics

Lifting-line model of a wind-turbine rotor

  • Wind energy
  • Aerodynamics
  • Vortex methods

Each blade carries bound vortices whose trailing filaments follow a prescribed helical wake; the circulation is iterated against airfoil polars until the loads agree. The solver returns spanwise loads, induction factors, and thrust and power coefficients, for the three-bladed example in under a second.

Scientific software · Unsteady aerodynamics

Vortex wakes of pitching and plunging airfoils

  • Aerodynamics
  • Vortex methods
  • Numerical methods

The airfoil is a row of vortex panels. At every time step the solver enforces zero normal flow and Kelvin’s circulation theorem, sheds a vortex from the trailing edge and lets the wake roll up freely. A thousand time steps take about two seconds.
